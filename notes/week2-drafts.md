## 19. Lyft, February 2024 · Tue 3 Nov

- **Move:** opened +23.5%, closed +35.1% ($12.13 → $16.39)
- **disguise:** A US transport app
- **mood:** The stock had fallen about 19% since the start of the year, well below where it traded a few months earlier. Investors wanted proof the company could grow and make money at the same time.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 1.22 expected / 1.22 actual; EPS ($) 0.08 expected / 0.19 actual
- **otherNews:** Gross bookings (the total value of all rides) rose 17% to $3.7bn. Its press release first said a key profit margin would grow by 500 basis points (5 percentage points) in 2024, which sent shares soaring after hours. On the evening call it corrected the figure to 50 basis points.
- **why:** Even after the typo was fixed, the real numbers were strong: earnings more than doubled what analysts expected and bookings beat forecasts. With expectations low after a weak start to the year, buyers kept piling in all day.
- **tag:** A real beat, not just a typo
- **source:** https://gazette.com/?p=89960

## 20. Chipotle, October 2025 · Tue 3 Nov

- **Move:** opened -21.4%, closed -18.2% ($39.76 → $32.53)
- **disguise:** A US fast-casual restaurant chain
- **mood:** The stock had already fallen about a third in 2025, after the company cut its sales forecast twice. Customer visits had dropped for two quarters in a row.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 3.03 expected / 3.0 actual; EPS ($) 0.29 expected / 0.29 actual; Same-store sales growth (%) 1.36 expected / 0.3 actual
- **otherNews:** It cut its full-year forecast for the third time this year: same-store sales (sales at restaurants open at least a year) are now expected to fall slightly, instead of staying flat. Customer visits fell again, and it said younger diners were coming less often.
- **why:** A third forecast cut showed the slowdown was getting worse, not better, and the company said sales were still weakening in October. It was the stock's worst day since 2012.
- **tag:** Third strike
- **source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/29/chipotle-cmg-earnings-q3-2025.html

## 21. Capital One, July 2025 · Tue 3 Nov

- **Move:** opened +4.8%, closed +0.9% ($217.42 → $219.46)
- **disguise:** A major US bank
- **mood:** The stock had risen about 22% in 2025 to close to its high, as investors bet on a huge merger it had completed two months earlier.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 12.22 expected / 12.49 actual; EPS, adjusted ($) 3.83 expected / 5.48 actual
- **otherNews:** Including one-off costs, it made a $4.3bn loss, mostly from setting aside $8.8bn to cover possible bad loans it took on in the merger. It also said the cost of combining the two companies would be higher than planned.
- **why:** Underlying profit easily beat forecasts, but the huge headline loss and rising merger costs left investors unsure what the combined bank would really earn. An early jump faded, and the stock ended almost flat.
- **tag:** Headline loss, hidden beat
- **source:** https://finviz.com/news/112043/cof-up-on-q2-earnings-beat-discover-deal-boosts-nii-and-fee-income

## 22. Gap, November 2024 · Wed 4 Nov

- **Move:** opened +17.1%, closed +12.8% ($22.04 → $24.87)
- **disguise:** A US clothing retailer
- **mood:** The company had already raised its forecast twice this year, but the stock had drifted sideways for three months heading into the all-important holiday season.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 3.81 expected / 3.83 actual; EPS ($) 0.58 expected / 0.72 actual
- **otherNews:** It raised its full-year sales forecast for the third time, to growth of 1.5–2% from 'up slightly', ahead of the 0.4% analysts expected. It also said the holiday season had got off to a strong start.
- **why:** A big profit beat and a third forecast raise right before the holidays sent the stock soaring at the open. Some of the jump faded during the day, but it still closed up 13%.
- **tag:** Third raise's the charm
- **source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2024/11/21/gap-gap-q3-2024-earnings.html

## 23. Ford, July 2024 · Wed 4 Nov

- **Move:** opened -13.7%, closed -18.4% ($13.67 → $11.16)
- **disguise:** A major US carmaker
- **mood:** The stock had risen about 12% in 2024, and investors hoped the company would raise its profit forecast after a strong start to the year.
- **rows:** Car revenue ($bn) 44.02 expected / 44.81 actual; EPS ($) 0.68 expected / 0.47 actual
- **otherNews:** Higher warranty costs (repairs it pays for on cars it has already sold), mostly on 2021 and older models, cut into profit. It kept its full-year profit forecast at $10–12bn instead of raising it.
- **why:** A big profit miss caused by repair bills on older cars showed its quality problems were still costing money, and keeping the forecast unchanged disappointed investors who had hoped for a raise. The stock slid further through the day.
- **tag:** Quality costs
- **source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/24/ford-motor-f-earnings-q2-2024.html

## 24. Monster Beverage, May 2025 · Wed 4 Nov

- **Move:** opened -2.2%, closed +1.4% ($60.14 → $61.00)
- **disguise:** A US drinks company
- **mood:** The stock had climbed more than 20% in three months, far ahead of other drinks companies, and was close to its highest level of the year.
- **rows:** Net sales ($bn) 1.98 expected / 1.85 actual; EPS ($) 0.46 expected / 0.47 actual
- **otherNews:** Sales fell 2.3%, which it blamed on how distributors timed their orders, a stronger dollar, bad weather and one fewer selling day. Its profit margin improved, and it said orders so far this year were up about 7% after adjusting for currency (excluding its alcohol brands).
- **why:** Sales missed badly, but the reasons looked temporary, profit still edged past forecasts and orders for the year were rising. The stock dipped at the open, then recovered to finish slightly higher.
- **tag:** Big miss, small shrug
- **source:** https://finviz.com/news/53803/monster-beverage-q1-earnings-beat-lower-sales-across-segments-hurt

## 25. Deckers, January 2025 · Thu 5 Nov

- **Move:** opened -12.1%, closed -20.5% ($223.11 → $177.36)
- **disguise:** A US footwear company
- **mood:** The stock had jumped about 38% in three months to its highest level in months, as investors bet its fast growth would continue.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 1.73 expected / 1.83 actual; EPS ($) 2.56 expected / 3.0 actual
- **otherNews:** It raised its full-year forecast, but the new numbers implied sales would fall next quarter for the first time in years, with thinner profit margins.
- **why:** The beat was big, but investors had priced in even more growth, and the forecast implied next quarter's sales would shrink with thinner margins. After a 12% drop at the open, selling continued all day.

- **source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/30/stock-market-today-live-updates.html

## 26. Mattel, July 2025 · Thu 5 Nov

- **Move:** opened -6.5%, closed -16.4% ($20.20 → $16.89)
- **disguise:** A US toymaker
- **mood:** The stock had risen about 28% in three months, as investors hoped it could cope with tariffs after it pulled its forecast in May because of trade uncertainty.
- **rows:** Net sales ($bn) 1.05 expected / 1.02 actual; EPS ($) 0.15 expected / 0.19 actual
- **otherNews:** Sales fell 6%, with North America down 16%, as retailers delayed orders because of tariffs and sales of its biggest doll brand slowed. It brought back a lower full-year forecast and said tariffs would cost it up to $100m before any fixes.
- **why:** Profit beat, but falling sales and a weaker outlook showed tariffs were already hurting, as stores held back on holiday orders. The stock kept sliding all day, ending down 16%.
- **tag:** Tariffs bite
- **source:** https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/article-mattel-sales-decline-weak-barbie-demand

## 27. Rivian, May 2024 · Thu 5 Nov

- **Move:** opened -7.2%, closed +0.2% ($10.25 → $10.27)
- **disguise:** A young US electric carmaker
- **mood:** The stock had crashed about 56% in 2024 so far, though it had bounced 13% in the two weeks before the results. Investors wanted signs it could cut costs.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 1.16 expected / 1.2 actual
- **otherNews:** Its loss widened to $1.45bn from $1.35bn a year earlier. But it kept its target of building 57,000 vehicles this year and cut its spending plan by $550m, to $1.2bn, by building its cheaper new model at its existing factory.
- **why:** The results were mixed: a wider loss pulled the stock down at the open, but the cost cuts and an unchanged production target meant no big surprise either way. By the close, the stock was flat.
- **tag:** Good and bad cancel out
- **source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/07/rivian-posts-quarterly-revenue-beat-cuts-2024-spending-forecast.html

## 28. Lululemon, December 2024 · Fri 6 Nov

- **Move:** opened +9.3%, closed +15.9% ($344.81 → $399.60)
- **disguise:** A premium athletic clothing brand
- **mood:** The stock had fallen about a third in 2024 as US sales slowed, but it had rallied 12% in the two weeks before the results.
- **rows:** Revenue ($bn) 2.36 expected / 2.4 actual; EPS ($) 2.69 expected / 2.87 actual
- **otherNews:** Sales grew 9%, driven by strong growth outside the US while US sales slowed. Its holiday forecast was in line with expectations, with the top of its profit range a little above what analysts expected.
- **why:** A clean beat and a steady holiday forecast reassured investors who had sold the stock all year, and buying kept building after the open. It closed up 16%.
- **tag:** Relief rally
- **source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/05/lululemon-lulu-earnings-q3-2024.html

## 29. Costco, March 2025 · Fri 6 Nov

- **Move:** opened -3.1%, closed -6.1% ($1,026.62 → $964.31)
- **disguise:** A big US discount retailer
- **mood:** The stock had risen 12% in 2025 and was close to its highs, as shoppers kept flocking to its stores.
